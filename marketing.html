CAMPAIGN BRIEF

SafeDial digital marketing plan

A practical starter plan for introducing the SafeDial emergency-access concept to South African audiences. This plan promotes a demonstration only; the sample codes do not connect to emergency services.

Planning note: the persona and targets below are working assumptions, not findings from customer interviews or keyword-volume tools. Validate them with research before spending campaign budget.

Target audience

  • Adults in South Africa who want safety information and trusted emergency contacts to be easier to find.
  • Families and caregivers discussing safety plans with relatives.
  • Community safety groups, local organisations and potential partners evaluating accessible safety tools.
  • Mobile-first users who prefer clear, concise information and may have limited data or time.

Sample customer persona

Nomsa, the prepared commuter

Profile: A fictional 29-year-old Cape Town commuter who travels early and late for shift work and helps coordinate safety check-ins with family.

Needs: Quickly find verified emergency numbers, keep family contacts accessible and understand what a safety app actually does.

Concerns: False promises, privacy, unreliable connectivity and unclear emergency instructions.

Message: “Know where to find help and who to contact. SafeDial is a concept preview, not an emergency service.”

Customer journey

  1. Awareness: Sees a social post about preparing trusted emergency contacts.
  2. Consideration: Visits About to understand the concept, limitations and values.
  3. Evaluation: Reviews sample codes and feature ideas on Services.
  4. Action: Uses Contact to send feedback or ask about a partnership.
  5. Retention and advocacy: Receives an email reply, shares the concept with a community group, or returns for updates.

SMART campaign objective

Within the first 30 days after the site is deployed and campaign tracking is configured, generate 300 visits to the Services page and receive 20 relevant feedback or partnership enquiries by publishing three educational social posts per week and using clear links to the Services and Contact pages. Review visits weekly and manually classify incoming emails; revise the target after establishing a baseline.

On-page SEO plan

  • Give each page its own descriptive title and concise meta description focused on its search intent.
  • Use one clear H1 per page, then descriptive H2 and H3 headings.
  • Use natural local terms such as “South Africa” where they accurately describe the page.
  • Keep page links descriptive and connect related About, Services and Contact content.
  • Write useful alt text for meaningful images; keep decorative backgrounds empty-alt.
  • Replace the homepage canonical placeholder with the verified live URL after deployment.
  • Compress images, test mobile layouts, add a sitemap and robots.txt, and verify pages in Google Search Console after launch.
  • Do not claim codes call real services; keep the emergency disclaimer visible on service content.
